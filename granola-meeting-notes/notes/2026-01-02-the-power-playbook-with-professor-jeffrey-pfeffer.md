## Summary

### Power Fundamentals

- Power determines what happens in the world and who does what
- Not about changing personality or temperament - it’s learnable behaviors and skills
  - Hard work, loyalty, and waiting your turn won’t get you promoted
  - People less talented will take opportunities if you don’t understand power
  - Especially useful for those facing extra hurdles due to systemic barriers
- Power = set of behaviors that can be learned through practice
  - Based on decades of social science evidence, not feel-good theories
  - Can be used for good or evil - required for all social change
  - Civil rights movement, medical practice changes all required power to disrupt status quo
- Getting power requires doing things that don’t come naturally
  - If it came naturally, everyone would do it and provide no advantage
  - Power is your ability to make things come out the way you want them to

### Job Selection and Career Strategy

- Person-job fit crucial beyond just skills
  - Does your persona fit the organization’s culture?
  - Do you have interpersonal style that makes you comfortable there?
  - Many people don’t last long due to cultural mismatch
- Critical job evaluation questions:
  - Do you have access to people at highest levels?
  - Will someone care about your performance based on role importance?
  - Is this considered relevant to organization’s success?
- Research strategy:
  - Google position title + company name to see what happened to predecessors
  - Ask about development opportunities and career progression
  - Avoid high-paid dead-end roles (high pay sometimes compensates for no future)
  - Watch for titles substituted for money or opportunity
- Always have exit plan (spend 10-15% time maintaining external options)
  - Better to find situations with higher success chance than fix current situation
  - Focus time on finding better fit rather than remedying poor fit
- Strategic department selection:
  1. Work in departments historically producing senior management
  2. Choose roles solving organization’s critical issues and bringing resources
  3. Ask: will this department become more or less important over time?
  4. “Be where the puck is going, not where it’s been”
  5. Seek positions providing visibility and boundary-spanning connections

### Physical Positioning and Organizational Dynamics

- Create power map of actual communication flows, not just org chart
  - Pay attention to people with access to leaders
  - Notice those controlling leaders’ calendars and time
  - Identify resource allocators regardless of hierarchy position
- Physical location matters significantly
  - Example: Stanford student chose desk in front of boss’s office over private office
  - Sitting where everyone wanting to see boss would see him
  - More contact with boss and relationship-building opportunities
- If senior management in office, you should be in office
  - Regardless of remote work policies
  - Someone else will build relationships if you’re not present
- In remote work, actively communicate accomplishments
  - Boss not thinking about you or your achievements
  - Your responsibility to ensure boss knows what you’re doing
  - Not bragging - it’s informing people about your capabilities

### Managing Up and First Impressions

- First 10 seconds determine perception for entire relationship
  - Nalini Ambadi study: 10-second silent video clips correlated with full-quarter course evaluations
  - People form impressions immediately that color everything afterward
  - If first impression is “brilliant,” everything coded as brilliant going forward
- Your most important day is your first day
  - Spend significant effort planning how to show up
  - First opportunity colors all subsequent interactions
- Boss promotion decisions based on:
  - Job performance (only one component)
  - How you make them feel about themselves
  - Whether they like you and see you as collaborative subordinate
- Maya Angelou principle: “People forget what you did, but never forget how you made them feel”
- Flattery as strategic tool:
  - Studies show no diminishing returns on flattery
  - Can be pure deceit and still effective
  - People motivated to believe wonderful things you tell them
  - 50%+ believe they’re above average on every positive trait
  - Use generously, particularly with people in power
- Criticism management:
  - Bosses hate being criticized (higher level = more sensitivity)
  - Frame changes as smart previous decisions needing updates due to circumstances
  - “As Kevin brilliantly suggested in prior meeting, we should do X”
  - Make your ideas seem like their ideas (people love their own ideas)

### Asking for Help and Building Confidence

- People underestimate others’ willingness to help
  - Columbia study: people needed to ask half as many strangers as expected
  - 20% refused to complete asking experiment due to discomfort
  - We’re trained to be self-reliant, making asking feel uncomfortable
- Asking for help is strategic relationship building
  - Elevates status of person being asked
  - Signals “you know something I don’t” - extremely flattering
  - Creates bonding opportunity through assistance
- Confidence and presence fundamentals:
  - People assume lack of competence if you demonstrate lack of confidence
  - Personal appearance matters: people respond to how you look, then sound, then content
  - Taller people more likely to become leaders; attractive people earn more
- Controllable appearance factors:
  - Footwear, wardrobe, colors that complement skin tone
  - Makeup, jewelry choices
  - Standing tall, initiating handshakes, moving toward people
  - Continuous eye contact, leaning toward conversation partner
- Speaking with confidence:
  - Use simple, short sentences without filler words
  - Avoid “I think I might have a point” - just make the point
  - Strategic pauses for emotional control and getting others to speak
  - Jack Valenti example: physically short but “feels tall” through presence
- Combat imposter syndrome:
  - Look at others in similar positions - are you really worse?
  - Someone else believed you deserved the role
  - Act confident and attitude will follow (attitudes follow behavior, not lead)

### Personal Branding and Professional Identity

- Strong brand key to success and differentiation
  - Brand = 2-4 sentence statement connecting past experiences to future goals
  - Not resume or biography - it’s compelling story of your journey
  - Example: Stanford student’s brand about bringing medical science to underserved St. Louis community
- Effective brand components:
  1. Personal history that makes you uniquely qualified
  2. Clear connection between experiences and aspirations
  3. Explanation of what makes you different from competitors
  4. “Elevator pitch” version - quick, compelling summary
- Brand dissemination strategies:
  - Social media posts aligned with expertise area (healthcare posts if healthcare expert)
  - Conference speaking opportunities
  - Blogging and article writing
  - Thought leadership positioning for status and differentiation
- Repetition crucial for brand recognition
  - Mercedes doesn’t advertise their brand just once
  - Tell your story repeatedly across multiple channels
  - If you don’t tell your story, someone else will or no one will
- Physical appearance as brand element:
  - Mark Zuckerberg’s gray hoodie
  - Jensen Huang’s leather jacket
  - Anna Wintour’s sunglasses
  - Consistency in look that ties to brand message
- Remote work considerations:
  - Zoom background helps define who you are
  - Personal portrait matters in virtual interactions
  - Brand can evolve like skills throughout career

### Strategic Networking and Relationship Building

- Networking positively related to power and influence advancement
- Required time investment: 6-8 hours per week (about 1 hour daily)
- Change time allocation: slightly less job execution, more relationship building
- Numbers game principle: more people you know, more opportunities available
- Strong vs. weak ties distinction:
  - Strong ties: frequent contact, deep emotional connection (family, close friends)
  - Weak ties: casual relationships with less frequent contact
  - Mark Granovetter’s “Getting a Job” study: best jobs found through weak ties
  - Weak ties provide non-redundant contacts, information, and opportunities
- Strategic networking approach:
  - Target people who know different things and expand your horizons
  - Don’t just network with comfortable, similar people
  - Like gym analogy: lift heavier weights to build strength
  - Extroversion not required - these are learnable skills
- Omar Shaya example: approached prominent CEO at airport, showed AI product on phone, CEO became company advisor
- Brokerage positions create substantial advantage:
  - Connect different groups and ideas to each other
  - Real estate agents connect buyers and sellers
  - VCs connect business ideas with capital
  - Networking as act of generosity through valuable connections
- Connection strategies:
  - Learn background information to build personal relationships
  - If executives play golf and you don’t, learn golf or find other connection points
  - Everyone has knowledge, wisdom, perspective to offer others
  - Most connected people have richest lives and opportunities

### Strategic Rule-Breaking and Power Dynamics

- Breaking rules means pushing boundaries of permitted/expected behavior
  - Sometimes defying social norms or doing unpermitted things
  - Essential for social change: Martin Luther King, Nelson Mandela
  - People who advance forge new paths, don’t just follow rules
- Strategic advantages of rule-breaking:
  1. Catches people by surprise - makes you notable and memorable
  2. Causes you to stand out from rule-followers
  3. People associate rule-breaking ability with having power
  - Dutch study: actors breaking small rules (taking extra space, eating extra cookie) perceived as more powerful and higher status
- Tactical rule-breaking examples:
  - Sit in different place at recurring meetings (especially head of table)
  - Take initiative for prominent positioning
  - Only break rules if you can avoid sanctions
  - Most people don’t push boundaries far enough due to conflict aversion
- Keith Ferrazzi example: Harvard MBA graduate negotiated dinner with Deloitte CEO once yearly as job condition
  - Broke chain of command and interview protocol
  - CEO accepted, became mentor/sponsor
  - Became youngest partner in firm history at 6 years
- Risk-return relationship in careers:
  - Safer choices = lower expected returns
  - Exceptional careers require exceptional (risky) actions
  - Never doing anything bold = no acceleration but probably no firing
- Never apologize principle:
  - Apologies don’t end problems, create more questions
  - “If you’re sorry, why did you do it? How did it happen?”
  - Makes you look weak and creates endless explanation cycles
  - Strong political figures don’t apologize
  - Alternative: “Thank you for waiting” instead of “Sorry I’m late”
- Strategic emotional management:
  - Niceness negatively correlated with salary for both genders
  - Nice people don’t negotiate hard for raises/promotions/resources
  - Avoid conflict even when healthy disagreement improves decisions
  - Refuse to ask for needed tools to avoid seeming pushy
- Appropriate anger display:
  - Anger signals high standards and power (Aristotle’s research)
  - Can be appropriate even toward boss, especially if boss is bully
  - Match emotional response to person you’re dealing with
  - Use variation - not angry all time, not nice all time
- Machiavelli’s principle: leader should be both feared and loved, but if choosing one, be feared
- Power acquisition: 20% granted, 80% taken through initiative and action

### Power Maintenance and Leadership Challenges

- Higher you rise, more intense competition becomes
- Skills shift from technical to power-focused as you advance
- Strategic rival management:
  - Willie Brown example: created Congressional seat for biggest rival Howard Berman
  - “Strategic outplacement” - get rivals fabulous jobs elsewhere
  - Don’t back rivals into corners (makes them fight harder)
  - Leave gracious exit routes to avoid ongoing hostility
- Time management and delegation:
  - Learn to say no to non-critical tasks and requests
  - Separate important from merely urgent demands
  - Can’t do everything as CEO - must delegate effectively
  - Avoid believing you know everything (power going to your head)
- Maintaining power through curiosity:
  - Stay intellectually curious about business, world, people
  - Most important quality for power retention
  - People lose power by “going to sleep” metaphorically
  - Never stop doing what got you into position initially
- Power’s psychological effects:
  - Leads to sense of disinhibition (“I can do whatever I want”)
  - Stop doing behaviors that brought them to power
  - Power becomes self-fulfilling as people come to your side
  - Condoleezza Rice: “Once people understand you can damage them, they come over to your side”
- Trump as power example:
  - Strong brand building, breaks rules, never apologizes
  - Demonstrates all principles discussed in class
  - Shows power dynamics playing out in real world
- Final guidance:
  - Use knowledge to advance causes that matter to you
  - Better observer of world around you even if don’t use for career advancement
  - “Don’t have wishbone where your backbone needs to be” (Clementine Paddleford)
  - Rise as high as you want with these tools
