## Summary

### Sprint Overview & Messaging Strategy

- First sprint for Certs Rip Pod focused on role-based, outcome-focused messaging approach
  - Shift from previous holistic outcomes messaging to individual-specific targeting
  - Previously: broad messaging around leadership upskilling (Navy SEALs) and innovation scaling (Moonshots)
  - Now: specific messaging for different seniorities and job functions with tailored outcomes
- Navy SEALs positioning: leadership skills development across management levels
  - Focus on building confidence, commanding authority, navigating high-stress situations
- Moonshots positioning: innovation and idea scaling for specific professional roles
  - Target vision-to-execution challenges, creative business problem-solving, scaling/pitching skills
- Critical differentiation requirement: avoid speaking same language as Core product
  - Different audience demographic and price point necessitate distinct messaging approach
  - Risk of ads feeling interchangeable with Core campaigns if not properly differentiated
- Messaging framework: transformational and forward-thinking without success guarantees
  - Focus on outcome potential and clarity rather than promised results

### Brand Guidelines & Copy Restrictions

- Google/Moonshot Factory naming protocol strictly enforced:
  - Cannot call them “Google” in any standalone context
  - Must use “Moonshot Factory” as primary brand identifier
  - Acceptable format: “Moonshot Factory, Google’s innovation lab”
  - Moonshot Factory name must always appear first before any Google reference
- Over-promising restrictions apply to both brands:
  - Prohibited language: “you’ll leave a leader,” “next big product,” guarantees of specific outcomes
  - Main partner concern: overly promissory statements about results
  - Focus on transformational language without concrete success promises
- Waymo reference complications for Moonshots:
  - Any Waymo mention (copy or imagery) triggers separate approval team
  - Additional 2-3 days added to standard review timeline
  - Standard moonshot review: typically 48 hours, max 3 days
- Navy SEALs Foundation context affects partnership dynamics:
  - Nonprofit organization with volunteer-only staff
  - Less participation and fewer resources compared to corporate partners
  - Asset creation avenue “pretty much exhausted” from previous attempts
- Partner review contractual framework: 5 days maximum, targeting 3-day turnaround
- Dani and Alex serve as primary liaison for brand compliance questions

### Target Audiences & Creative Direction

- Navy SEALs three-tier seniority approach:
  - Emerging managers: newly promoted individuals struggling with team leadership
    - Pain points: commanding authority, building confidence in management role
    - Outcomes: learning to command the room, establishing leadership presence
  - Mid-level managers: experienced managers seeking executive advancement
    - Pain points: transitioning from management to executive-level responsibilities
    - Outcomes: developing executive-level leadership capabilities
  - Senior level/directors: established leaders managing complex situations
    - Pain points: navigating high-stress environments, managing teams during chaos
    - Outcomes: mastering crisis leadership and team management under pressure
- Moonshots three-function professional targeting:
  - Product managers: vision-to-execution gap challenges
    - Pain points: struggling to move from concept to real product development
    - Outcomes: turning ideas into executable product strategies
  - UX designers: creative thinking for broader business impact
    - Pain points: limited systems for solving larger business challenges beyond design
    - Outcomes: developing frameworks for creative business problem-solving
  - Entrepreneurs/founders: scaling and investor engagement
    - Pain points: having ideas but lacking scaling knowledge or investor pitch skills
    - Outcomes: learning to scale concepts and effectively present to investors
- Creative direction mandate: lifestyle imagery prioritized over action-focused content
  - Rationale: more control over approval process and asset availability
  - Approach: contextual shots showing audience demographics (younger person for emerging managers, etc.)
  - Execution: people in realistic professional/personal contexts rather than abstract concepts

### Imagery Requirements & Restrictions

- Navy SEALs imagery verification workflow with Alex Bollinger:
  - All images must be shared with Alex before use
  - Alex coordinates with military team member for authenticity verification
  - Critical requirement: images must be actual Navy SEALs, not generic military/action shots
  - Getty/stock libraries often incorrectly tag non-SEALs content as SEALs imagery
  - Weapons explicitly prohibited in all Navy SEALs creative
- Abstract military object alternatives explored:
  - Macro shots of knots, compass, uniform elements acceptable
  - Rationale: abstract objects with strong headlines can create leadership connections
  - Examples: compass imagery linking to leadership direction, knot imagery suggesting strength
- AI generation guidelines vary by content type:
  - Navy SEALs: avoid AI for action photos, lifestyle generation acceptable with context
  - Moonshots: previous AI lifestyle imagery performed poorly (basic “lady in chair” concepts)
  - AI images require thoughtful prompting to avoid generic/contextless results
- Getty Images usage concerns:
  - Stock imagery often overused, partners may reject due to brand association risks
  - Cannot legally manipulate Getty images through AI tools like Midjourney
  - High-performing existing ads mostly use Getty, limiting reuse options
- Certificate imagery alternative approach:
  - Can work across both Navy SEALs and Moonshots campaigns
  - Provides more design control while maintaining professional credibility
- Text-only creative performance data:
  - Historical poor performance eliminates pure text ads as viable option
  - Design elements acceptable (infographics, graphical components) if not purely textual
  - Example: ads with design elements like geometric shapes performed at 70% effectiveness
- Underwater/diving imagery results mixed:
  - Some Navy SEALs diving creative achieved scale but faced legal complications
  - One scuba diver shot required pause due to rights issues
  - Generally lower traction compared to action-focused alternatives

### Deliverables & Timeline Structure

- Total creative asset calculation: 54 unique pieces
  - 9 unique ads per brand (Navy SEALs and Moonshots)
  - Each unique ad produced in 3 format variations = 27 per brand
  - Combined total: 54 creative assets across both campaigns
- Unique ad breakdown per brand:
  - 3 audience segments × 3 ads per segment = 9 unique concepts per brand
  - Each concept requires distinct headline, subhead, and imagery combination
- Timeline framework: 2.5-week sprint structure
  - 2 weeks dedicated creative development (10 working days)
  - 0.5 week (3 days) allocated for partner review process
  - Buffer accounts for potential review extensions within contractual limits
- Copy-first approval workflow implementation:
  - Send copy for partner approval before design execution
  - Rationale: prevents design rework if copy changes required during review
  - Robinson can begin visual exploration during copy approval without full commitment
- Creative development methodology:
  - Benji generates 5-7 concept options per audience segment
  - Team collaboratively narrows to 3 hero concepts for production
  - Staggered delivery approach as team establishes workflow with new messaging
- Copy specifications aligned with existing brand standards:
  - Headlines: 4-8 words optimal length
  - Subheads: approximately 100 characters maximum
  - Punctuation guidelines: periods for multi-sentence headlines, none for single sentences
- Performance benchmarking considerations:
  - Differentiated creatives perform better than headline-only variations
  - Same imagery with different headlines shows limited scaling potential
  - Lifestyle imagery historically underperformed compared to action shots for both brands
- CTA integration learnings from previous campaigns:
  - Pre-enrollment offer ribbons (15% discount) significantly outperformed standard ads
  - Urgency messaging combined with visual CTAs drove higher performance
  - Current challenge: no active promotional offers available for CTA integration
  - Visual noise concern with multiple elements (logos, copy, CTA components)
