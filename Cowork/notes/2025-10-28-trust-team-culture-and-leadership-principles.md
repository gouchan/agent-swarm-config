## Summary

### Trust as Foundation

- Built through practice and consistency over time
  - Confidence and trust develop naturally through conversations and open dialogue
  - Requires belief in people you’re working with
- F1 pit stop analogy: team must trust driver to stop precisely at 80km/h
  - All four tires changed in sub-2 seconds
  - 2.5-3 second delay loses critical track time (difference between first and last)
  - Driver must brake perfectly to avoid “knocking them out like bowling pins”
  - Team gets closer and more on edge as trust builds through practice
- Childhood trust fall game: closing eyes, knowing others will catch you
  - Circle of people, one person in center falls backward
  - Complete faith others will prevent hitting ground
- Key elements of feeling trusted:
  - Reliability: “you show up,” “you’re there for me”
  - Physical and emotional support: “you have my back,” “I can lean on you”
  - No abandonment during vulnerable moments (coming out, medical conditions)
  - Won’t be rejected, laughed at, or judged for deeply personal revelations
- Mutuality and reciprocity essential
  - Not just about revealing, but how you respond
  - “It’s ok, I’m here, I’m not going anywhere”
  - “You can do this,” “I appreciate it,” “do it again”
  - Eyes and words convey “you’re in safe territory”
- Paradox: “more risks you take, safer you feel; safer you feel, more risks you take”

### Trust in Leadership and Communication

- Takes time to build, lost instantly, creates “army of trustworthy people”
  - More people you build trust with, stronger your support network
  - Trusted people “always give leader benefit of the doubt”
  - Will “follow you wherever you say”
- Employees must trust leader’s:
  - Truthfulness in all communications
  - Decision-making quality over time
  - Course of action choices for the unit
- High-level trust required that leader truly believes what they’re saying
- Message intended to help regardless of whether recipient likes it
- “I’m just the messenger here, delivering the message to you”
- One-on-one relationship building with each team member essential
- Worst scenario: teams skeptical of everything leader says, reluctant to follow
- Unit confidence rises when trust in leadership solidifies
- Keeping unit together long-term enables “magical things to happen”

### Staying Through Difficulty

- Natural human instinct to bail during stressful situations
  - “We’ve all done it too” - uncomfortable moments leading to abandonment
  - Extremely difficult to “hang in” when things get tough
- “Bailing” extremely hard to recover from in relationships
- Greatest employee/friend memory: not abandoning during failure
  - “It’s what employees remember, what friends remember”
  - Staying when “going down together on sinking ship”
  - “No greater joy because you’re not alone”
  - Having witness to failure creates deep bond
- Stage performance anxiety example:
  - Moving from regular person to performing creates speech paralysis
  - Having someone “on stage with you, in work with you, in life with you”
  - Those who don’t bail “you really will love them forever”
- SNL example: delighting in watching cast stay in character
  - “Held on to that fucking bronco until it rode out of town”
  - Pride in being someone who “had not left the room, physically or spiritually”
- Personal standard: “I will excuse almost any behavior except for bailing”

### Team Interdependence and Collective Responsibility

- Fundamental principle: when one part fails, whole team fails
  - Not just the failing part that suffers consequences
  - “Everybody fails” when team coordination breaks down
- Must have support of entire team regardless of individual role
- “We rely on each other, count on each other, cover and move for each other”
- Cover and move combat strategy application:
  - One team advances while other provides suppressive fire
  - If either team fails their part, “results can be disastrous”
  - Business requires same absolute coordination for mission success
- Individual ego management through unified identity:
  - Olympic team approach: told Kobe, LeBron, Chris Paul to “bring egos in”
  - “Let’s make sure they’re all under one umbrella” - USA ego
  - “You are not playing for United States” - shocking initial statement
  - Gold medal won’t happen “unless you are United States basketball”
  - “We’re asking you to be, we’re asking you to own it”
- 15 team standards development:
  - Standards vs rules: “standard is yours,” “rule you never own”
  - Individual meetings with players to ensure contribution
  - LeBron’s first standard: “no excuses”
  - Other standards: no complaints, look each other in eye, tell truth, have each other’s back, never late, never bad practice
  - “It was our practice, our deal, not Coach K’s practice”

### Building Strong Culture and Encouraging Innovation

- Culture definition: understanding human behavior to elevate groups beyond individual selves
  - Each person has responsibility to shape organizational behavior
  - Behavior becomes foundation for company culture, values, guiding principles
- Dinner table test: employees proud enough to share company values at home
  - “How many people are so proud of company, its values, what it stands for”
  - Sharing with family “how proud they are to be part of this enterprise”
  - “How much they respect what this company stands for”
- Swift correction when actions contradict core values
  - Especially critical in early company stages
  - Ensure everyone understands inconsistent actions “not consistent with values”
  - “We are not going to do it or going to stop doing it”
- Key cultural elements for success:
  - Trust and empowerment: employees feel empowered to make mistakes, do job their way, not be micromanaged
  - Clear goals: people know what they’re aiming for, how individual goals attach to greater goals
  - Purpose-driven contribution understanding
  - Shared wins and celebration with great acknowledgement
  - Whole person interest: caring about entire person, not just work role
  - Results in “exponentially more” from employees
- Role complementarity over equality:
  - “Good team has good complementarity of roles”
  - “I can be who I am because you are filling missing pieces”
  - Equity more important than equality in relationships
  - Hierarchical fairness: proper decision-making authority for assigned roles
- Collaborative fit requirements:
  - Open to new ideas, good listener
  - “Commonality of lanes without giving up your lane”
  - Open-minded acceptance of others’ views and beliefs
  - Operator + product developer example: “jive together” creating “uncustomed collision between disciplines”
- Innovation through failure encouragement:
  - “Only way to encourage creativity” is giving room to fail
  - Leader must “shoulder that” when risks don’t work
  - People too tentative without failure safety net
  - “Great failure might teach something leading to great next success”
  - Some companies have “rewards for great failures of the year”
- Personal failure reframing example:
  - Father asked at dinner: “what did you guys fail at this week?”
  - Disappointment when no failures to report
  - “Way to go” high-fives for trying and being horrible
  - Redefined failure as about not trying, not poor outcomes
- Spanx test and learn culture:
  - “Oops meetings” with entire company
  - Leaders model by sharing their mistakes publicly
  - Theme songs for mistakes (Britney Spears “Oops I Did It Again”)
  - Makes everyone “feel more relaxed to make a mistake”
  - Essential for maintaining “highly innovative, rapidly evolving company”
  - People’s jobs can’t be at risk for messing up or lose competitive edge
