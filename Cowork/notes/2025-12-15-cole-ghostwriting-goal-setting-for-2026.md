### Ghostwriting Goal Framework - Data Tracking Requirements

- Fundamental rule: “What gets measured gets improved”
  - Cannot set goals without measurement systems
  - Most writers have information problems, not action problems
- Seven essential weekly metrics to track:
  1. **Outreach messages sent** (outbound lead generation)
    - Primary driver for new client conversations
    - Starting point: If sending zero, start with one next week
    - Progressive doubling: 1→2→4→8→16 messages
  2. **Content pieces posted** (inbound attraction strategy)
    - Alternative to outreach for client acquisition
    - Can focus on one approach or combine both
    - Direct correlation: More content = more inbound inquiries
  3. **Free consulting Looms per week**
    - Research prospect’s specific challenges
    - Record 5-minute educational video addressing their problem
    - Not salesy or spammy approach - purely helpful positioning
  4. **Follow-ups completed**
    - “Every single person here has one writing client somewhere”
    - Client exists in: DMs, email, phone texts, friend/family network
    - Most common failure: Not following up consistently
  5. **Inbounds received** (output metric)
  6. **Free consulting calls booked** (output metric)
  7. **New clients acquired** (ultimate output metric)
- Reality check on current tracking
  - Question posed: “Who currently tracks all these things?”
  - Result: Handful of yes responses, majority tracking nothing
  - No need for complex Notion dashboards or Zapier automations

### Goal Setting Strategy - Pick One & Double It

- Single-focus approach prevents habit formation failure
  - Mistake: Trying to improve all metrics simultaneously
  - Successful strategy: Choose one input metric to double in January
  - Examples of doubling targets:
    - 10 weekly outreach messages → 20 messages
    - 2 weekly content pieces → 4 pieces
    - 1 weekly consulting Loom → 2 Looms
    - 2 follow-ups → 4 follow-ups
- Understanding input-output relationships
  - Outputs don’t happen independently
  - To increase inbounds: Must increase content creation (input)
  - To increase consulting calls: Must increase outreach + Looms (inputs)
  - “If you want an output to go up, you have to go up on the input”
- Follow-up philosophy: Persistence until explicit rejection
  - “Follow up until they call the cops”
  - Continue until receiving message: “Please stop contacting me or I will call authorities”
  - Student example: Sandy landed client after 13 follow-ups
  - Cole’s approach: Monthly follow-ups for 2+ years until clients ready
- Reframing persistence as helpfulness
  - Business owners wake up to 100+ Slack messages, chaotic email/texts
  - Helpful messaging examples:
    - “Hey Cole, I’m sure you’re really busy. Just bumping this up in your inbox”
    - “I’m sure you’re swamped. I just wanted you to see this. By the way, I came up with another idea for your newsletter”
  - Perception shift: Looks accountable and trustworthy, not desperate
  - Addresses common faulty beliefs about appearing annoying or desperate

### What Needs to Change - Behavior Modification Requirements

- Core question: “Why haven’t you doubled this metric yet?”
  - Time passage doesn’t solve fear or hesitation
  - Tomorrow’s fear level identical to today’s without intervention
  - Something must change to achieve different results
- Four categories requiring change:
  1. **Mindset shifts**
    - Address fear-based thinking preventing action
    - Challenge limiting beliefs about outreach/follow-up
  2. **Behavior modifications**
    - Current approach may be unproductive
    - Need more effective execution methods
  3. **Schedule restructuring**
    - Creating content twice weekly requires time reallocation
    - Must reorganize calendar to accommodate doubled activity
  4. **Effort level increases**
    - Doubling output inherently requires more work
    - Accept increased energy/attention investment
- Specific change examples (not vague improvements):
  - “Wake up 5am instead of 6am every Monday for extra hour”
  - “Increase personalization and care in each outreach message”
  - “Block one extra afternoon per week for content creation”
  - “Cut one night of Netflix weekly for ghostwriting activities”
  - “Use lunch breaks more productively for business development”
  - “Block Sunday morning time for weekly planning”
- Framework characteristics: Small, actionable changes
  - Not complete life overhauls or dramatic shifts
  - Manageable adjustments to existing routine
  - Goal setting meaningless without attached behavior change
