## Summary

### Trust as Currency

- Trust currency = ultimate pass in negotiations
  - More trust in client’s bank = greater willingness to take risks
  - Built through constant communication and touchpoints
  - Caring about everything from apartment appearance to injury treatment
- Eric Bledsoe turned down $28M first offer because trust was established
  - Took phone calls on “thousand random nights”
  - Rich stood by him when “world was against me”
  - Trust building basics:
    - Do what you say (follow through shows reliability)
    - Be honest about limits (builds credibility)
    - Offer visibility (walk them through how you got to your number)

### Survey the Landscape

- “The room is wherever your feet are” - could be car, boardroom, train
- Key preparation questions:
  - Who’s really calling the shots?
  - What do they want or fear losing?
  - Understanding timing and market conditions
- 2014 NBA context: salary caps and franchise values climbing
  - Business of basketball was “bubbling”
  - Knew eventually someone would say “if you don’t want this guy, we’ll take him”
- Talent scarcity = most powerful negotiating position

### Lead with Value, Not the Ask

- Provide impact beyond your role
- Show worth before stating price
- Eric Bledsoe’s value to Phoenix:
  - Closing games on both ends of floor
  - Great teammate, teammates loved him
  - Exciting player who lifted team morale
- Market value vs. team-specific value often different
  - Your value to specific team can exceed market value
  - “You are worth what you negotiate”

### Anchoring Strategy

- Anchoring = making the first move to set positioning
- “It’s not about the number” - number creates automatic ceiling
- Don’t negotiate “like you buy a bed in a bag” - feel your way through
- Jalen Johnson example:
  - Set first anchor at $175M (max based on value)
  - Team’s first offer: $110M ($65M apart)
  - Final deal: $150M (came down one notch, they came up 3-4 notches)
- When to anchor first vs. let them lead:
  - Anchor when you know market, want control, have strong strategic number
  - Let them lead when you don’t know full picture or they have more leverage

### Know Your Worth

- Most important evaluator of your value = you
- Must put in work to justify claiming high value
- “If you ever get fired, your value is how fast your phone rings”
- Sometimes being underpaid temporarily acceptable for future positioning
- Key evaluation factors:
  - Research market value
  - Highlight what sets you apart
  - Track salary vs industry trends
- Use AI to check market and prep for smart moves

### Reading People and Situations

- Acknowledgment first step in reading someone
- Watch for stress signals that reveal deal importance:
  - Crossed arms = resistance
  - No eye contact = uncertainty or hiding something
  - Fidgeting/tapping/shifting = tension
  - Pauses/stammering = internal conflict
- Face-to-face negotiation essential
  - Can see “the truth” in person vs. text/email
  - Body language says yes before words do
- Manage your own emotions - stay “emotionless” like when gambling
  - Don’t show excitement or disappointment
  - “Most powerful place to be: any answer is fine with me”

### Control the Narrative

- Tristan Thompson example: $38M to $82M final deal
  - Media narrative: not worth max contract money
  - Used Draymond Green’s $82M deal as benchmark
  - Strategically leaked near-final number to shift perception
- “Perception is power” - shape how deal is seen
- Stage your presentation like staging a house
  - Accentuate positives with factual analytics
  - Address deficiencies by pivoting back to strengths
- Social proof and testimonials help maximize value positioning

### Closing Tactics

- “Take it or leave it” requires:
  - Knowing your value
  - Understanding decision maker’s temperament
  - Knowing timeline and positioning
  - Having alternatives/leverage
- Tyrese Maxey deal: waited for max value ($204M vs. early $140M offers)
- “Most powerful negotiation position: be willing to walk away”
- Eric Bledsoe final moves:
  - Moved furniture out, put house for sale
  - Clear message: “if you don’t want us, we don’t want to be here”
  - Final deal: 5 years, $70M (nearly triple first $28M offer)
