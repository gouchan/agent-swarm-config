## Summary

### FCU Application Process and Timeline

- Applications due January 31st - no benefit to applying early
  - Will not read applications until after January 31st deadline
  - Applying early actually detrimental as less time for quality preparation
  - Only first submission reviewed if multiple applications sent
  - Wait until close to deadline to maximize preparation time
- 500-word cover letter is creative copywriting task, not biographical summary
  - Common mistake: starting with “I first knew I wanted to be a writer when I was X years old” (used by \~20% of 500 previous applicants)
  - Should engage reader from first line - “do I want to read the next line?” test
  - Pattern disruption and storytelling preferred over biographical details
  - Treat as copywriting exercise to demonstrate skills
- Writing sample optional - only submit if confident in quality
  - Does not need to be financial copy
  - Better to submit nothing than poor-quality work
- 12-week unpaid program starting February 23rd
  - Weekly calls at 9:30 ET for 1-1.5 hours
  - Flexible schedule around existing work commitments
  - Not school-style attendance requirements
  - Designed to work around client obligations
- Four students from previous cohort received full-time MarketWise offers
  - Access to royalty program included
  - Duncan Moreland testimonial highlights career transformation

### Ideal Candidate Profile

- Creative application approach over biographical details
  - Don’t focus on wanting to be copywriter - assumption if applying
  - Tell story, engage, show something surprising
  - Example: Previous candidate mailed one shoe with “getting foot in the door” letter
  - Another sent toilet seat (less successful approach)
  - Logic behind unusual approach matters more than gimmick itself
- Demonstrates work ethic and commitment
  - First year will be very difficult with self-doubt periods
  - Bell curve of emotions: initial optimism, reality setting in, eventual success
  - Need proof of ability to learn new skills and handle unglamorous work
  - Industry often misrepresented as easy job anyone can do anywhere
  - Reality: requires tremendous dedication, time, and commitment
- Show why you’re interesting rather than trying to persuade
  - Focus on engagement and storytelling ability
  - Creativity and spark more important than existing knowledge base
  - Hard work and endeavor can fill knowledge gaps

### Copy Development and Feedback Structure

- Weekly copy pod meetings with 7-8 writers for idea feedback
  - Comfortable setting for immediate feedback and idea strengthening
  - Can then bring refined ideas to editors and publishers
- Ideas always considered, not always accepted
  - Four different marketing calendars requiring substantial copy volume
  - Willing to test unconventional approaches historically rejected by industry
  - Examples of successful innovations:
    - $40M bond promotion (industry said “can’t sell bonds”)
    - Healthcare copy success
    - Format changes and experimental approaches
  - Done with extensive feedback, scaffolding, and mentorship
- Heavy focus on idea generation and development
  - Core differentiator for successful copywriters
  - Most students shocked by intensity of idea interrogation
  - Process from basic idea to headline expression extensively developed
  - Key element preventing copywriters from breaking through to real-world success
- Example headline development process:
  - “50-year Wall Street legend” concept tested for emotional impact
  - Market selling off but message inherently bullish
  - Bearish headline designed to capture bearish viewers while pivoting to bullish message
  - Credit to senior writer Allison Camato (came through similar program with no experience)

### Work Structure and Responsibilities

- Primarily VSLs/webinars as centerpiece content
  - Long copy element remains core focus for moving the needle
  - Still write lift notes, sales pages, and secondary materials
  - Top-of-funnel promotion work most impactful
- Co-writing common between Nick and Kelly for secondary materials
- 101-level lessons included covering:
  - Direct response copywriting fundamentals
  - Structures and lift note basics
  - Complete coverage for newcomers to industry
- Global remote work permitted
  - Can work anywhere (US subsidiary question confirmed)
  - Previous requirement to relocate to Baltimore office no longer exists

### AI and Industry Perspective

- AI replacing bad writers, not affecting top-tier copywriters
  - No evidence of changes at top end of market
  - If client says AI can do better, indicates copy quality issues
  - Most AI promotion by “snake oil salesmen” selling courses
- Usage recommendations and observations:
  - Useful for research and pulling analysis from sales letters
  - Not recommended for actual writing or short-form content
  - Performance analysis shows writers using AI more heavily have declining sales numbers
  - Risk of growing too dependent and weakening core writing muscle
  - Great writers can use AI for speed and generating test variations
  - Still requires human skill to select best options from AI output
- MarketWise approach:
  - Not building AI models for copywriting work
  - Investing significant time and money in human copywriter training
  - Weeks of direct mentorship investment demonstrates belief in human approach
